What is RNA?

RNA, or Ribonucleic Acid, is a crucial molecule that plays a vital role in how genetic information is used in living cells. While DNA stores genetic information, RNA helps to read and execute those genetic instructions.

Key Characteristics of RNA

  • Structure: Unlike DNA's double helix, RNA is typically single-stranded and more flexible. It contains ribose sugar instead of deoxyribose.
  • Bases: RNA uses four bases: Adenine (A), Guanine (G), Cytosine (C), and Uracil (U) - replacing Thymine found in DNA.
  • Main Functions: RNA serves multiple critical roles in cells, including carrying genetic instructions from DNA to protein-making machinery, and helping to create proteins.

Types of RNA

There are several important types of RNA:

  1. Messenger RNA (mRNA): Carries genetic instructions from DNA to ribosomes for protein creation.
  2. Transfer RNA (tRNA): Helps transport amino acids during protein synthesis.
  3. Ribosomal RNA (rRNA): Forms part of the ribosomes where proteins are made.

You can think of RNA as a messenger and worker in the cell, translating the genetic code stored in DNA into the proteins that perform most of life's functions.
