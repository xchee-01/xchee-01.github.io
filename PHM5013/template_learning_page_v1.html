1 What are Covalent Bonds?

Covalent bonds form when two atoms share one or more pairs of electrons. Unlike ionic bonds, where electrons are transferred, covalent bonding involves the mutual sharing of electrons between atoms, creating a strong and stable connection.

2 Key Principles

Electron Sharing

Atoms share valence electrons to achieve a stable electron configuration, typically following the octet rule.

Bond Strength

Covalent bonds are strong, requiring significant energy to break. Bond strength varies with bond order.

Directionality

Covalent bonds have specific orientations in space, determining molecular geometry and properties.

Reversibility

In drug design, covalent bonds can be reversible or irreversible, affecting drug duration and selectivity.

4 Relevance to Drug Discovery

Covalent bonds play a crucial role in drug-target interactions:

  • Covalent Inhibitors: Form permanent or reversible bonds with target proteins
  • Prodrug Activation: Covalent bond cleavage releases active drug molecules
  • Drug Metabolism: Enzymatic reactions involve covalent bond formation and breaking
  • Target Selectivity: Specific covalent interactions enhance drug specificity
