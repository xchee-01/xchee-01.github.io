📊 Calculating Key Statistics

# Calculate the statistical story of our patient cohort mean_chol = np.mean(cholesterol_levels) std_chol = np.std(cholesterol_levels) median_chol = np.median(cholesterol_levels) # Percentiles tell us about distribution percentile_25 = np.percentile(cholesterol_levels, 25) percentile_75 = np.percentile(cholesterol_levels, 75) print(f"\n📊 Statistical Summary:") print(f"Mean: {mean_chol:.1f} mg/dL") print(f"Median: {median_chol:.1f} mg/dL") print(f"Std Dev: {std_chol:.1f} mg/dL") print(f"IQR: {percentile_25:.1f} - {percentile_75:.1f} mg/dL")
Copied!
Learning Checkpoint
Which statistical measure is most resistant to outliers?
Mean (np.mean)
Not quite. The mean can be heavily influenced by outliers.
Standard deviation (np.std)
That's not right. Standard deviation is also affected by outliers.
Median (np.median)
Correct! The median is the middle value when data is sorted and is not affected by extreme values (outliers).
Range (max - min)
Not correct. The range is directly determined by outliers (the extreme values).

🚨 Risk Stratification

# Identify patients needing intervention (>200 mg/dL is elevated) high_risk = cholesterol_levels > 200 borderline = (cholesterol_levels >= 180) & (cholesterol_levels <= 200) optimal = cholesterol_levels < 180 # Create a risk report risk_percentage = (np.sum(high_risk) / len(cholesterol_levels)) * 100 print(f"\n🚦 Risk Stratification:") print(f"🟢 Optimal (<180): {np.sum(optimal)} patients") print(f"🟡 Borderline (180-200): {np.sum(borderline)} patients") print(f"🔴 High risk (>200): {np.sum(high_risk)} patients ({risk_percentage:.0f}%)") # Statistical significance check if risk_percentage > 50: print("\n⚠️ Alert: Majority of cohort at elevated risk!") print("📋 Recommendation: Implement clinic-wide lifestyle intervention program")
