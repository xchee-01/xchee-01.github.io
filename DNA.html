Introduction to DNA

What is DNA?

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a molecule composed of two chains that coil around each other to form a double helix carrying genetic instructions for the development, functioning, growth and reproduction of all known organisms and many viruses.

Key Facts About DNA

  • DNA was first identified and isolated by Friedrich Miescher in 1869
  • The double helix structure was discovered by Watson and Crick in 1953
  • DNA is made of four types of nucleotides: Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Guanine (G), and Cytosine (C)
  • The human genome contains approximately 3 billion base pairs

DNA Structure

The Double Helix

DNA's structure is described as a double-stranded helix, with the two strands connected by hydrogen bonds. Each strand has a backbone made of alternating sugar (deoxyribose) and phosphate groups.

Nucleotide Pairing

DNA follows strict base-pairing rules: Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T), and Guanine (G) pairs with Cytosine (C). This complementary base pairing is crucial for DNA replication and protein synthesis.
