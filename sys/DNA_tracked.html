What is DNA?

DNA, or Deoxyribonucleic Acid, is the fundamental blueprint of life. It's a complex molecule that carries the genetic instructions used in the growth, development, functioning, and reproduction of all known living organisms.

Key Features of DNA

DNA is often described as a double helix, resembling a twisted ladder. It consists of:

  • Nucleotides: The basic building blocks of DNA, composed of a sugar, a phosphate group, and a nitrogen-containing base.
  • Base Pairs: The rungs of the DNA ladder, where specific bases always pair together: Adenine (A) with Thymine (T), and Cytosine (C) with Guanine (G).
  • Genetic Code: The specific sequence of these bases determines the genetic instructions for an organism.

Every cell in your body contains DNA, which acts like a unique genetic fingerprint. It determines everything from your eye color to your potential health risks, passing down traits from parents to children.

DNA Replication

When cells divide, DNA must be replicated so that each new cell gets a complete set of genetic instructions. This process is remarkably accurate, with error rates as low as one mistake per billion base pairs. Here's how it works:

  1. The DNA double helix unwinds, with the two strands separating.
  2. Each original strand serves as a template for building a new complementary strand.
  3. Nucleotides pair up with their matching bases on each template strand.
  4. The result is two identical DNA molecules, each containing one original strand and one new strand.

This semi-conservative replication ensures genetic continuity across generations of cells.
