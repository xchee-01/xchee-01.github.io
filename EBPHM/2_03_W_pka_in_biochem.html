1. Zwitterionic Form

1.1. Definition and Formation

  • Definition: A zwitterion is a molecule with both positive and negative charges
  • Formation:
    • At physiological pH, the amino group is protonated (-NH₃⁺)
    • The carboxyl group is deprotonated (-COO⁻)
    • This creates a dipolar ion or zwitterion

1.2. Charge Distribution

  • α-amino group: positively charged at pH < 9
  • α-carboxyl group: negatively charged at pH > 2
  • Side chain: may be charged depending on its pKa and the pH

1.3. Significance

  • Contributes to amino acid solubility in water
  • Affects interactions with other molecules
  • Influences protein structure and function

Note: The zwitterionic nature of amino acids explains why they can behave as both acids and bases, and contributes to their unique chemical properties.

2. Isoelectric Point (pI)

2.1. Definition

The pH at which the amino acid carries no net charge

2.2. Calculation

  • For amino acids with no ionizable side chains: pI = (pKa₁ + pKa₂)/2
  • For amino acids with ionizable side chains: depends on the relative pKa values

2.3. Significance

  • At pI, amino acids have minimum solubility in water
  • Basis for isoelectric focusing in protein purification
  • Proteins precipitate at their pI

Note: The isoelectric point is a crucial parameter in protein separation techniques like electrophoresis and chromatography, as it determines the pH at which a protein will not migrate in an electric field.

3. pKa Values of Amino Acid Functional Groups

3.1. α-Carboxyl Groups

  • Typical pKa range: 1.8 - 2.4
  • Generally more acidic than isolated carboxylic acids (pKa ~4.5)
  • Reason for increased acidity: The adjacent positively charged α-NH₃⁺ group stabilizes the negative charge of the carboxylate
  • Variation among amino acids: Small differences due to side chain effects

3.2. α-Amino Groups

  • Typical pKa range: 8.8 - 10.8
  • Generally less basic than isolated amines (pKa ~10.5)
  • Reason for decreased basicity: The adjacent negatively charged α-COO⁻ destabilizes the positive charge on the ammonium
  • Variation among amino acids: Influenced by proximity and nature of side chains

3.3. Side Chain Functional Groups

3.3.1. Acidic Side Chains

  • Aspartic acid (Asp, D):
    • Side chain: carboxylic acid (-COOH)
    • pKa: ~3.9 (more acidic than α-COOH)
    • Charged at physiological pH: Yes, negative (-COO⁻)
  • Glutamic acid (Glu, E):
    • Side chain: carboxylic acid (-COOH)
    • pKa: ~4.1 (more acidic than α-COOH)
    • Charged at physiological pH: Yes, negative (-COO⁻)

3.3.2. Basic Side Chains

  • Lysine (Lys, K):
    • Side chain: ε-amino group (-NH₂)
    • pKa: ~10.5
    • Charged at physiological pH: Yes, positive (-NH₃⁺)
  • Arginine (Arg, R):
    • Side chain: guanidino group
    • pKa: ~12.5 (very basic)
    • Charged at physiological pH: Yes, positive
  • Histidine (His, H):
    • Side chain: imidazole group
    • pKa: ~6.0 (near physiological pH)
    • Charged at physiological pH: Partially (exists in both protonated and deprotonated forms)
    • Special significance: Acts as both acid and base in enzyme catalysis

3.3.3. Polar Side Chains (Weakly Ionizable)

  • Cysteine (Cys, C):
    • Side chain: thiol (-SH)
    • pKa: ~8.3
    • Charged at physiological pH: Mostly no, but some thiolate (-S⁻) exists
  • Tyrosine (Tyr, Y):
    • Side chain: phenolic hydroxyl (-OH)
    • pKa: ~10.1
    • Charged at physiological pH: No (predominantly protonated)
  • Serine (Ser, S) and Threonine (Thr, T):
    • Side chain: hydroxyl (-OH)
    • pKa: ~13-16 (very weakly acidic)
    • Charged at physiological pH: No
