Basic Structure

  • General Formula: H2N-CαHR-COOH

    • General amino acid structure showing central alpha carbon with four groups attached.

  • Central Carbon (Cα): Also called the α-carbon
    • Tetrahedral structure
    • Forms the backbone of all amino acids
    • Connects four different groups:
      1. Amino group (-NH2)
      2. Carboxyl group (-COOH)
      3. Hydrogen atom (-H)
      4. Side chain (-R)
    • Joined together peptide bond formation through condensation reaction

Types of Carbons in Amino Acids

  • Alpha-carbon (Cα): The central carbon to which the amino group, carboxyl group, hydrogen, and side chain are attached
  • Beta-carbon (Cβ): The first carbon in the side chain, directly attached to Cα
  • Gamma-carbon (Cγ): The second carbon in the side chain
  • Delta-carbon (Cδ): The third carbon in the side chain
  • Epsilon-carbon (Cε): The fourth carbon in the side chain

Stereochemistry of Amino Acids

  • Chiral Center: The α-carbon is a chiral center in all amino acids except glycine
    • Four different groups attached to Cα create asymmetry
    • Glycine has two hydrogens, making it achiral
  • Enantiomers: Two possible spatial arrangements around the α-carbon
    • D-configuration (dextrorotatory)
    • L-configuration (levorotatory)
  • L-Configuration in Biological Systems:
    • Almost all naturally occurring amino acids in proteins are L-amino acids
    • In L-amino acids, the α-carbon has:
      • Amino group (-NH2) on the left
      • Carboxyl group (-COOH) at the top
      • Side chain (-R) on the right
      • Hydrogen atom (-H) at the bottom

Note: Understanding the stereochemistry of amino acids is crucial for comprehending protein structure and function, as the specific three-dimensional arrangement of amino acids determines how proteins fold and interact with other molecules.
